# 神農本草經 上經 003 玉泉 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yuquan (jade spring). Sweet, neutral. Governs the hundred illnesses of the five zang. It softens the sinews and strengthens the bones, calms the hun and po, grows the flesh and augments qi. Taken long, one endures cold and heat (quotations in the *Taiping yulan* often write 耐 "endure" as 能; in antiquity they were interchangeable), is neither hungry nor thirsty, does not age and becomes an immortal. If a dying person takes five jin, his colour does not change three years after death. Also called yuzha (the *Taiping yulan* quotes it as yunong. The *Chuxue ji* quotes: "Jade peach: taking it, one lives long and does not die." The *Taiping yulan* also quotes: "Jade peach: taking it, one lives long and does not die. If one cannot take it early, taking it on the day one approaches death, the corpse will not decay until heaven and earth come to an end." Thus 杜 is suspected to be an error for 桃). It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Yuquan, also called yuxie (jade powder). Shennong, Qibo, Leigong: sweet; Mr Li: neutral. It fears donghua and abhors green bamboo (*Taiping yulan*). The du of white jade is like baitougong (ibid. The *Shilei fu* quotes: "the body of white jade is like a white-headed old man").

## Notes

The entry on yuquan, the jade taken as an immortality drug.
- The parentheses in the main text are Sun's notes. Which 杜 his remark 杜疑當作桃 refers to is not clear from the text (perhaps 白玉杜 in Wu Pu's quotation); the translation keeps the characters.
- Sun's note (案) is not translated; it cites the *Zhouli* on eating jade and the *Baopuzi* on preparing jade.
