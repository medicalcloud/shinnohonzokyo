# 神農本草經 上經 016 青石、赤石、黄石、白石、黒石脂等 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Blue, red, yellow, white and black shizhi (halloysite clays), etc. Sweet, neutral. Govern jaundice, diarrhoea, intestinal afflux with pus and blood, genital erosion, bleeding with red and white discharge, evil qi, welling-abscesses and swellings, flat-abscesses and haemorrhoids, malign sores, scalp sores and scabies itch. Taken long, they supplement the marrow and augment qi, make one plump and strong and free of hunger, lighten the body and prolong the years. The five shizhi each supplement the five zang according to their five colours. They grow in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: The five-coloured shizhi, also called the blue, red, yellow, white and black [shizhi] etc. Qingfu: Shennong: sweet; Leigong: sour, non-toxic; Tongjun: acrid, non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly cold. It grows on the Southern Mountains or by the seashore; collected at no fixed time. Chifu: Shennong, Leigong: sweet; Huangdi, Bian Que: non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly cold. It grows on Shaoshi or on Mount Tai; deep red in colour and slippery like fat. Huangfu: Mr Li: slightly cold; Leigong: bitter. It may grow on Mount Song; the colour is like pig's brain or a gosling; collected at no fixed time. Baifu, also called suisui: Qibo, Leigong: sour, non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly cold; Tongjun: sweet, non-toxic; Bian Que: acrid. It grows on Mount Tianlou of Shaoshi or on Mount Tai. Heifu, also called shini: Tongjun: sweet, non-toxic. It grows on open ground in the mountains west of the Luo.

The *Mingyi* says: It grows on the sunny side of the Southern Mountains (one edition reads Nanyang). It also says: heishizhi is also called shinie or shimo. Note: the *Wu Pu* cites Shennong's "sweet" and so on; each of the five shizhi had its own entry, and later generations combined them into one. The *Fanzi jiran* says: "Chishizhi comes from Hedong; the red is good." The *Liexian zhuan* says: "Chixuzi liked to eat shizhi."

## Notes

The entry on the five-coloured shizhi (wuse shizhi). In the Zhongyi Jicheng text it has no heading and follows the zishiying entry, but it corresponds to wuse shizhi in the list of names and is treated as a unit.
- Shizhi are clayey minerals (chishizhi is an iron-bearing clay).
- The part from "Note" in the *Mingyi* paragraph is Sun's comment; since it stands in the same paragraph in the base, it is translated.
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition reads 疥掻 (variant).
