# 神農本草經 上經 019 菖蒲 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Changpu (shobu; acorus). Acrid, warm. Governs wind-cold-damp impediment and cough with counterflow and rising qi. It opens the apertures of the heart, supplements the five zang, opens the nine orifices, brightens the ears and eyes and brings out the voice. Taken long, it lightens the body; one does not forget, is not confused, and the years are prolonged. Also called changyang (the *Taiping yulan* quotes: "That which grows on rocks with nine nodes per cun, taken long, lightens the body, etc." The *Daguan* edition lacks the three characters "grows on rocks" and has "that with nine nodes per cun is good", in black characters). It grows in pools and marshes.

The *Wu Pu* says: Changpu, also called yaojiu (the *Yiwen leiju* quotes "also called changyang").

The *Mingyi* says: It grows in Shangluo and Yandao of Shu Commandery. The root is collected on the twelfth day of the fifth month and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on changpu (acorus), the first upper-grade herb.
- 開心孔 is understood as opening the apertures of the heart, i.e. clearing consciousness and thought.
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 昌蒲 (variant).
