# 神農本草經 上經 020 鞠華 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Juhua (chrysanthemum flower). Bitter, neutral. Governs wind, dizziness of the head with swelling and pain, eyes [aching] as if about to fall out, tearing, dead skin, aversion to wind and damp impediment. Taken long, it benefits the blood and qi, lightens the body, lets one withstand ageing and prolongs the years. Also called jiehua. It grows in rivers and marshes and in fields.

The *Wu Pu* says: Juhua, also called baihua (*Chuxue ji*), nühua or nüjing.

The *Mingyi* says: Also called rijing, nüjie, nühua, nüjing, gengsheng, zhouying, fuyannian and yincheng. It grows in Yongzhou. The root is collected in the first month, the leaves in the third, the stems in the fifth, the flowers in the ninth and the fruit in the eleventh month; all are dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on juhua (chrysanthemum flower). Sun uses the archaic writing 鞠華 for the heading.
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 菊花 (variant).
- Sun's notes (including the paragraph citing Guo Pu) are not translated.
