# 神農本草經 上經 023 甘草 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Gancao (licorice). Sweet, neutral. Governs cold and heat and evil qi of the five zang and six fu. It firms the sinews and bones, grows the flesh, doubles strength, [treats] swelling from metal wounds and resolves toxins. Taken long, it lightens the body and prolongs the years (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "also called meicao or migan"; the *Daguan* edition has it in black characters). It grows in river valleys.

The *Mingyi* says: Also called migan, meicao, micao or lucao. It grows on Mount Jisha in Hexi and in Shangjun. The root is collected on the chu days of the second and eighth months and dried in the sun; it is ready in ten days.

## Notes

The entry on gancao (licorice).
- 尰 means swelling. The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition reads 腫 and "grows in mountain valleys" (variants).
