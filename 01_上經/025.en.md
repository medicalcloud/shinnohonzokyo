# 神農本草經 上經 025 朮 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zhu (atractylodes). Bitter, warm. Governs wind-cold-damp impediment, dead flesh, tetany and jaundice. It stops sweating, dispels heat and disperses food. It is made into a decoction and taken as food. Taken long, it lightens the body, prolongs the years and prevents hunger. Also called shanji (the *Yiwen leiju* quotes shanjin). It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Zhu, also called shanlian, shanjie, tiansu or shanjiang (*Yiwen leiju*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called shanjiang or shanlian. It grows on Mount Zheng, in Hanzhong and Nanzheng. The root is collected in the second, third, eighth and ninth months and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on zhu (atractylodes). The Classic does not distinguish baizhu and cangzhu.
- Sun's note (案) is not translated (it cites the *Shanhai jing*, *Erya*, *Baopuzi*, etc.).
