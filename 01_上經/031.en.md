# 神農本草經 上經 031 柴胡 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chaihu (saiko; bupleurum). Bitter, neutral. Governs [illnesses of] the heart and abdomen; it removes bound qi in the intestines and stomach, accumulations of food and drink, and cold and heat and pathogenic qi, and pushes out the old to bring in the new. Taken long, it lightens the body, brightens the eyes and augments essence. Also called dixun.

The *Wu Pu* says: Zihu, also called shancai or rucao. Shennong, Qibo, Leigong: bitter, non-toxic. It grows in Yuanju. The root is collected in the second and eighth months (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called shancai or rucao. The leaves, also called yunhao, are acrid, fragrant and edible. It grows in Hongnong and Yuanju. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on chaihu (bupleurum).
- The main text has no clause on where it grows.
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 茈葫 (variant).
