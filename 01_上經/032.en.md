# 神農本草經 上經 032 麦門冬 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Maimendong (ophiopogon). Sweet, neutral. Governs bound qi in the heart and abdomen, damage to the centre and damage from overeating, severance of the network vessels of the stomach, and emaciation with shortness of breath. Taken long, it lightens the body, prevents ageing and hunger. It grows in river valleys and on embankment slopes.

The *Wu Pu* says: Also called majiu, xindong, rendong, renling, busiyao, pulei or suizhi (the *Taiping yulan* quotes: "also called yangjiu; in Qin also called majiu or yujiu; in Yue also called yangqi, maijiu, yujiu, xinjiu or yuyuliang"). Shennong, Qibo: sweet, neutral; Huangdi, Tongjun, Leigong: sweet, non-toxic; Mr Li: sweet, slightly warm; Bian Que: non-toxic. It grows on fertile ground in mountain valleys. The leaves are like Chinese chives, fleshy, glossy and growing in clumps. Collected at no fixed time. The fruit is bluish-yellow.

The *Mingyi* says: In Qin it is called yangjiu, in Qi maijiu, in Chu majiu and in Yue yangshi; also called yujia or yuyuliang. The leaves are like Chinese chives and grow all winter and summer. It grows in the fertile soil of Hangu and in long-abandoned places among rocks. Collected in the second, third, eighth and tenth months and dried in the shade.
