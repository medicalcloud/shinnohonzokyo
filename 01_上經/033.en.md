# 神農本草經 上經 033 独活 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Duhuo (dokukatsu; angelica pubescens). Bitter, neutral. Governs being struck by wind-cold and metal wounds, and stops pain; [it governs] running piglet, epilepsy and tetany, and mounting-conglomeration in women. Taken long, it lightens the body and lets one withstand ageing. Also called qianghuo, qiangqing or huqiang shizhe. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Duhuo, also called huwang shizhe. Shennong, Huangdi: bitter, non-toxic. Collected in the eighth month. This drug does not move when there is wind and sways by itself when there is no wind (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called huwang shizhe or duyaozhe. This plant does not sway in the wind and moves by itself without wind. It grows in Yongzhou or in Nan'an of Longxi. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on duhuo; the Classic gives qianghuo as one of its other names.
- 一名拡羌使者 in the base is emended to 護羌使者 (see the emendation).
- 花 in Wu Pu's 有風花不動 seems superfluous; it is translated "does not move when there is wind" (not verified).
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition reads 奔豚 and 癇痓 (variants).
