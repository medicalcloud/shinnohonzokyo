# 神農本草經 上經 036 署予 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shuyu (shoyo; Chinese yam) (the old editions write 薯蕷; the *Taiping yulan* writes 署豫, which is correct). Sweet, warm. Governs damage to the center; it supplements deficiency and emaciation, dispels cold and heat and pathogenic qi, supplements the center, augments vigor and grows the flesh. Taken long, the ears and eyes become keen, the body is lightened, one is not hungry and the years are prolonged. Also called shanyu. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Shuyu, also called zhushu (the *Taiping yulan* writes 署予 and 諸署; the *Yiwen leiju* also writes 諸). In Qi and Yue it is called shanyu, also called xiucui or ercao (the *Taiping yulan* quotes: "In Qin and Chu it is called yuyan, in Qi and Yue shanyu, in Zheng and Zhao shanyu, also yuyan"). Shennong: sweet, slightly warm; Tongjun, Leigong: sweet (the *Taiping yulan* quotes bitter), non-toxic. It may grow on Mount Zhong in Linqu. When it first grows it has red stems and thin vines; in the fifth month the flowers are white; in the seventh month the fruit is bluish-yellow and in the eighth month ripens and falls. The root is white inside with yellow skin, like taro (the *Taiping yulan* quotes: "the root is collected in the second and eighth months; it abhors gansui [kanzui]").

The *Mingyi* says: In Qin and Chu it is called yuyan, in Zheng and Yue tuzhu. It grows on Mount Song. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on shuyu (Chinese yam, shanyao). Sun names it 署豫 after the *Taiping yulan*.
- The base repeats 長肌肉; the repetition is removed as superfluous (see the emendation).
