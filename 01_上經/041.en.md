# 神農本草經 上經 041 細辛 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Xixin (asarum). Acrid, warm. Governs cough with counterflow, headache with throbbing of the brain, hypertonicity of the hundred joints and wind-damp; [it governs] impediment pain and dead flesh. Taken long, it brightens the eyes, frees the nine orifices, lightens the body and lengthens the years. Also called xiaoxin. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Xixin, also called xicao (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "also called xiaoxin"). Shennong, Huangdi, Leigong, Tongjun: acrid, slightly warm; Qibo: non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly cold. It is like mallow leaves, reddish-black, with each root joined to a single leaf (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "the root is collected in the third and eighth months").

The *Mingyi* says: It grows in Huayin. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on xixin (asarum).
- The base punctuates 風湿。痺痛、死肌; it could also be read continuously as 風湿痺痛，死肌. The translation follows the base punctuation.
