# 神農本草經 上經 055 紫芝 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zizhi (purple ganoderma). Sweet, warm. Governs deafness; it frees the joints, protects the spirit, augments the essential qi, firms the sinews and bones and improves the complexion. Taken long, it lightens the body, prevents ageing and prolongs the years. Also called muzhi. It grows in mountain valleys (the old editions have six kinds; now they are combined).

The *Wu Pu* says: Zizhi, also called muzhi.

The *Mingyi* says: Chizhi grows on Mount Huo, heizhi on Mount Heng, qingzhi on Mount Tai, baizhi on Mount Hua and huangzhi on Mount Song; zizhi grows on the ground of Gaoxia, purple in colour and shaped like mulberry (*Taiping yulan*). The six zhi are all non-toxic and are collected in the sixth and eighth months.

## Notes

The entry on zizhi, the last of the six zhi; the places of growth common to the six are recorded here.
- 舊作六種，今並 means that Sun counted the six zhi as one item (hence the count of seventy-three upper-grade herbs in the tally).
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition reads 久食 (variant).
