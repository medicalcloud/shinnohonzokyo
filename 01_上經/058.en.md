# 神農本草經 上經 058 芎藭 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Xiongqiong (ligusticum). Acrid, warm. Governs wind-stroke entering the brain, headache, cold impediment, cramping, slackening and tensing of the sinews, metal wounds, and blood blockage with childlessness in women. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Xiongqiong (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "also called xiangguo"). Shennong, Huangdi, Qibo, Leigong: acrid, non-toxic; Bian Que: sour, non-toxic; Mr Li: warm when fresh, cold when cooked. It grows on the shady side of Mount Huwutao or on Mount Tai (the *Taiping yulan* reads "the western ridge of Xiegu, or Mount Tai"). The leaves are fragrant, fine and bluish-black, with red veins like gaoben (kohon); it grows in clumps in winter and summer. The flowers are red in the fifth month, the fruit black in the seventh month, with two leaves at the end of the stem. Collected in the third month. The root has nodes and is shaped like a horse's bit.

The *Mingyi* says: Also called huqiong or xiangguo. Its leaves are called miwu. It grows on the western ridge of Xiegu in Wugong (goko). The root is collected in the third and fourth months and dried in the sun.
