# 神農本草經 上經 061 絡石 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Luoshi (rakuseki; star jasmine). Bitter, warm. Governs wind-heat, dead flesh, abscess injuries, dry mouth and parched tongue, welling-abscesses and swellings that do not disperse, swelling of the throat and tongue, and inability to get water and fluids down. Taken long, it lightens the body, brightens the eyes, moistens and improves the complexion, prevents ageing and prolongs the years. Also called shiling. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Luoshi, also called linshi, mingshi, xuanshi, yunhua, yunzhu, yunying or yundan. Shennong: bitter, slightly warm; Leigong: bitter, non-toxic; Bian Que, Tongjun: sweet, non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly cold. It is called a sovereign among drugs. Collected at no fixed time (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called shicuo, lüeshi, mingshi, lingshi or xuanshi. It grows on the shady side of Mount Tai or rocky mountains, or on rocks of high mountains, or among human dwellings. Collected in the first month.

## Notes

The entry on luoshi (Trachelospermum).
- 水漿不乾 in the base is emended to 水漿不下 ("water and fluids do not go down").
