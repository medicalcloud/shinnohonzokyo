# 神農本草經 上經 064 肉松蓉 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Rousongrong (cistanche). Sweet, slightly warm. Governs the five taxations and seven damages; it supplements the centre, removes cold, heat and pain in the penis, nourishes the five zang, strengthens yin, augments the essential qi and multiplies offspring. [It governs] concretions in women. Taken long, it lightens the body. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Roucongrong, also called rousongrong. Shennong, Huangdi: salty; Leigong: sour, slightly warm (the *Taiping yulan* reads "Mr Li: slightly warm"). It grows on the shady ground of mountains in Hexi (the *Taiping yulan* reads "east"), three or four cun long, in clumps; or in Dai Commandery (the *Taiping yulan* adds "Yanmen"). Collected from the second to the eighth month (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "dried in the shade before use").

The *Mingyi* says: It grows in Hexi and in Yanmen of Dai Commandery. Collected on the fifth day of the fifth month and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on rousongrong (roucongrong, cistanche); Sun's heading is 肉松蓉.
- 茎中 is understood as the inside of the penis.
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 肉松容 (variant).
