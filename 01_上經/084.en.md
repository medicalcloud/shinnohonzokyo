# 神農本草經 上經 084 沙参 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shashen (shajin; adenophora). Bitter, slightly cold. Governs blood accumulation and fright qi; it dispels cold and heat, supplements the center and augments the lung qi. Taken long, it benefits a person. Also called zhimu (chimo). It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Baishashen, also called kuxin, shimei, huxu, baishen, zhiqu or wenhu. Shennong, Huangdi, Bian Que: non-toxic; Qibo: salty; Mr Li: slightly cold. It grows in the river valleys of Henei or on Mount Du in Banyang. It sprouts in the third month, like mallow, with blue-green leaves; the fruit is white like mustard seed and the root large and white like turnip. Collected in the third month (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called kuxin, zhiqu, huxu, baishen, shimei or wenxi. It grows in Henei, Yuanju and on Mount Xu in Banyang. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on shashen (adenophora). The Classic gives zhimu as another name (different from the middle-grade zhimu).
