# 神農本草經 上經 101 枸杞 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Gouqi (kuko; lycium). Bitter, cold. Governs pathogenic qi of the five internal [organs], heat in the center with wasting-thirst, and generalized impediment. Taken long, it firms the sinews and bones, lightens the body and prevents ageing (the *Taiping yulan* reads "withstands ageing"). Also called qigen, digu, gouji or difu. It grows in plains and marshes.

The *Wu Pu* says: Gouqi, also called gouji (written 己) or yangru (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called yangru, queshu, xianren zhang or Xiwangmu zhang. It grows on Mount Chang and on hills, slopes and banks. The root is collected in winter, the leaves in spring and summer, and the stems and fruit in autumn; dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on gouqi (lycium), whose root (digu), leaves and fruit are used.
- The alternative name 枸杞 in the base is emended to 枸忌 (see the emendation).
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition reads 軽耳 (variant), probably an error.
