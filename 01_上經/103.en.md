# 神農本草經 上經 103 伏苓 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Fuling (bukuryo; poria). Sweet, neutral. Governs counterflow qi in the chest and ribs (the *Taiping yulan* reads "mounting qi"), anxiety and anger, fright pathogen and fear, palpitations with bound pain below the heart, cold and heat with vexation, fullness and cough with counterflow, and parched mouth and dry tongue; it frees urination. Taken long, it calms the hun, nourishes the spirit, prevents hunger and prolongs the years. Also called futu (the *Taiping yulan* reads fushen. Note: the original edition says, "That which embraces the root is called fushen", in black characters). It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Fuling gives access to the spirits. Tongjun: sweet; Leigong, Bian Que: sweet, non-toxic. It may grow under the roots of large pines in Maozhou, three zhang one chi in the ground. Collected on the seventh day of the second month (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: That which embraces the root is called fushen. It grows under large pines on Mount Tai. Collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on fuling (poria); Sun writes the heading 伏苓.
- 恐 ("fear") is supplied in the base's 驚邪，悸 (see the emendation).
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 茯苓 (variant).
