# 神農本草經 上經 120 牛黄 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Niuhuang (goo; cattle bezoar). Bitter, neutral. Governs fright epilepsy, cold and heat, and exuberant heat with mania and tetany; it dispels evil and drives out ghosts. It grows in plains and marshes.

The *Wu Pu* says: Niuhuang is bitter and non-toxic. It is found in cattle that groan whenever they go in and out (the *Taiping yulan* reads "low and bellow"). It glows at night (the *Taiping yulan* reads "seen at night it glows") and runs inside the horns (the *Taiping yulan* has the character 牛); when the ox dies it enters the gallbladder, like egg yolk (commentary on the biography of Yan Du in the *Hou Han shu*).

The *Mingyi* says: It comes from the land of Jin. Obtained from cattle, it is dried in the shade for a hundred days, turned from time to time and not exposed to the light of sun or moon.

## Notes

The entry on niuhuang (cattle gallstone).
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 狂痓 (variant).
- 使時躁 in the *Mingyi* quotation probably means "turn (or dry) it from time to time", but this is unclear.
