# 神農本草經 上經 137 大棗 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dazao (jujube). Sweet, neutral. Governs evil qi of the heart and abdomen; it calms the centre and nourishes the spleen, assists the twelve channels, levels the stomach qi, opens the nine orifices, supplements shortage of qi, scant fluids and insufficiency within the body, great fright and heaviness of the four limbs, and harmonizes the hundred drugs. Taken long, it lightens the body and lengthens the years. Its leaves, covering mahuang, can make one sweat. It grows in plains and marshes.

The *Wu Pu* says: Jujube governs regulating the centre; it augments the spleen qi, improves the complexion and enhances the will and qi (the *Wu shi bencao* as quoted in the *Daguan bencao*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called ganzao, meizao or liangzao. Collected in the eighth month and dried in the sun. It grows in Hedong.

## Notes

The entry on dazao (jujube).
- 肋十二経 in the base is emended to 助十二経 ("assists the twelve channels").
- 葉覆麻黄，能令出汗 is understood to mean that the leaves, used together with mahuang, induce sweating.
