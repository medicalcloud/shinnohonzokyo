# 神農本草經 上經 137 大棗 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dazao (taiso; jujube). Sweet, neutral. Governs pathogenic qi of the heart and abdomen; it calms the center and nourishes the spleen, assists the twelve meridians, levels the stomach qi, opens the nine orifices, supplements shallow breathing, scant fluids and insufficiency within the body, great fright and heaviness of the four limbs, and harmonizes the hundred drugs. Taken long, it lightens the body and lengthens the years. Its leaves, covering mahuang (mao), can make one sweat. It grows in plains and marshes.

The *Wu Pu* says: Jujube governs regulating the center; it augments the spleen qi, improves the complexion and enhances the will and qi (the *Wu shi bencao* as quoted in the *Daguan bencao*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called ganzao, meizao or liangzao. Collected in the eighth month and dried in the sun. It grows in Hedong.

## Notes

The entry on dazao (jujube).
- 肋十二経 in the base is emended to 助十二経 ("assists the twelve meridians").
- 葉覆麻黄，能令出汗 is understood to mean that the leaves, used together with mahuang, induce sweating.
