# 神農本草經 上經 142 麻蕡 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Mafen (hemp flower). Acrid, neutral. Governs the five taxations and seven damages; it benefits the five zang, brings down blood and [treats] cold qi. Eaten in quantity, it makes one see ghosts and run about madly. Taken long, it gives access to the spirit light and lightens the body. Also called mabo. Mazi (hemp seed): sweet, neutral; governs supplementing the center and augmenting qi, makes one plump and strong, prevents aging and makes one an immortal. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: The kernel of hemp seed: Shennong, Qibo: acrid; Leigong, Bian Que: non-toxic. It does not want oyster shell or baiwei (byakubi). That which has first been stored in the ground kills a person if eaten. Malan, also called mafen, qingyu or qingge: Shennong: acrid; Qibo: toxic; Leigong: sweet. It fears oyster shell and baiwei. The leaves carry toxin and kill a person if eaten. Mabo, also called hua: Leigong: acrid, non-toxic. It fears oyster shell (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Mabo is the fluffy matter on hemp flowers. It is best collected on the seventh day of the seventh month. The seeds are collected in the ninth month. It grows on Mount Tai.

## Notes

The entry on mafen (hemp flower) and mazi (hemp seed).
- The base heading 麻賁 is emended to 麻蕡.
- "Seeing ghosts and running madly" when eaten in quantity probably describes the psychoactive effect of cannabis.
