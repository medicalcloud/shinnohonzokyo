# 神農本草經 中經 023 芍薬 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyao (peony root). Bitter, neutral. Governs evil qi with abdominal pain; it removes blood impediment, breaks hard accumulations, cold and heat and mounting-conglomeration, stops pain, frees urination and augments qi (the *Yiwen leiju* quotes "also called baizhu"; the *Daguan* edition has it in black characters). It grows in river valleys and on hills.

The *Wu Pu* says: Shaoyao. Shennong: bitter; Tongjun: sweet, non-toxic; Qibo: salty; Mr Li: slightly cold; Leigong: sour. Also called ganji, jiecang, dan, yurong or baizhu. Collected on the third day of the third month (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called baizhu, yurong, lishi, jieshi or ting. It grows on the Central Peak. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on shaoyao (peony root). The Classic does not distinguish red and white peony.
- The alternative name baizhu is not the same as baizhu (atractylodes).
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 邱陵 (variant).
