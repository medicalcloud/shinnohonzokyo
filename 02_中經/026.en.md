# 神農本草經 中經 026 元参 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yuanshen (scrophularia). Bitter, slightly cold. Governs cold and heat and accumulations in the abdomen and lingering illnesses after childbirth in women; it supplements the kidney qi and brightens the eyes. Also called chongtai. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Yuanshen, also called guicang, zhengma, chongtai, lufu, duan or yuantai. Shennong, Tongjun, Huangdi, Leigong, Bian Que: bitter, non-toxic; Qibo: salty; Mr Li: cold. It may grow in Shanyang of Yuanqu. It puts out leaves in the second month, downy like plum, set in fours like peony; the stems are black and square, four or five chi tall. The flowers are red, growing between the branches; the fruit is black in the fourth month (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called yuantai, luchang, zhengma, jian or duan. It grows in Hejian and Yuanju. The root is collected in the third and fourth months and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on yuanshen (xuanshen, scrophularia); 元 replaces 玄 to avoid a Qing taboo.
- 産乳餘疾 refers to illnesses remaining after childbirth.
