# 神農本草經 中經 033 黄芩 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangqin (ogon; scutellaria). Bitter, neutral. Governs the various heats and jaundice and intestinal afflux with diarrhea; it expels water and brings down blood blockage, and [treats] malign sores, erosive ulcers [base: 恒蝕] and burns. Also called fuchang. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Huangqin, also called huangwen, dufu, hongsheng, hongqin, yintou or neixu. Shennong, Tongjun, Huangdi, Leigong, Bian Que: bitter, non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly warm. In the second month it puts out red-yellow leaves, facing each other in twos and fours; the stem is hollow, square or round, three or four chi tall. In the fourth month the flowers are purplish-red, in the fifth month the fruit is black; the root is yellow. Collected from the second to the ninth month (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called kongchang, neixu, huangwen, hongqin or dufu. It grows in Zigui and Yuanju. The root is collected on the third day of the third month and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on huangqin (scutellaria).
- 恒蝕 is translated following 疽蝕 in the Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition (the base is kept); that text also writes 黄岑 (variants).
