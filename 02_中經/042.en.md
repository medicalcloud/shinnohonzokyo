# 神農本草經 中經 042 紫参 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zishen (shisan). Bitter, acrid, cold. Governs accumulations in the heart and abdomen and cold, heat and pathogenic qi; it opens the nine orifices and frees urine and stool. Also called mumeng. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Fumeng, also called zishen, quanrong, yinfu, futu or chongshang. Shennong, Huangdi: bitter; Mr Li: very cold. It grows in the mountain valleys of Hexi or on Mount Shang in Yuanju. It grows in round clusters; the root is yellow-red with moon-like markings [?], the skin black and the inside purple. In the fifth month the flowers are purplish-red, the fruit black and as large as beans. The root is collected in the third month (abridged from the *Taiping yulan* and the *Daguan* edition).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called zhongrong, tongchang or maxing. It grows in Hexi and Yuanju. The root is collected in the third month and roasted over fire to make it purple.
