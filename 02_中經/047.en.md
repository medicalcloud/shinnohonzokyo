# 神農本草經 中經 047 水萍 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shuiping (suihyo; duckweed). Acrid, cold. Governs violent heat with itching of the body (the *Yiwen leiju* and *Chuxue ji* write 癢, which is correct); it drains water qi, overcomes wine, grows the beard and hair (the *Yiwen leiju* reads "blackens the hair") and [treats] wasting and thirst. Taken long, it lightens the body. Also called shuihua (the *Yiwen leiju* quotes "also called shuilian"). It grows in pools and marshes.

The *Wu Pu* says: Shuiping, also called shuilian. It grows on the water of marshes. The leaves are round and small, one leaf to a stem, with roots entering the water. The flowers are white in the fifth month; collected in the third month and dried in the sun (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called shuibai or shuisu (suiso). It grows in Leize. Collected in the third month and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on shuiping (duckweed).
- 一始水白 in the *Mingyi* quotation is taken as an error for 一名水白.
