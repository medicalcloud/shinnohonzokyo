# 神農本草經 中經 054 牡丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Mudan (tree peony root bark). Bitter, acrid, cold. Governs cold and heat, wind-stroke with tugging and slackening and tetany, and fright epilepsy with evil qi; it removes hard concretions and static blood lodged in the intestines and stomach, calms the five zang and treats abscesses and sores. Also called lujiu or shugu. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Mudan. Shennong, Qibo: acrid; Mr Li: slightly cold; Leigong, Tongjun: bitter, non-toxic; Huangdi: bitter, toxic. The leaves are set like fleabane; the root is black like arborvitae, with a core inside. Collected in the second month and in the eighth month and dried in the sun. Eaten, it lightens the body and increases longevity (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: It grows in Ba Commandery and Hanzhong. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on mudan (mudanpi, moutan bark).
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition reads 瘈瘲 and 癥腎 (variants; 腎 is probably an error).
