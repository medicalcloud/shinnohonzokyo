# 神農本草經 中經 070 秦皮 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qinpi (shinpi; ash bark). Bitter, slightly cold. Governs wind-cold-damp impediment and shivering cold qi; it dispels heat and [removes] blue nebulae and white membranes in the eyes. Taken long, the hair does not turn white and the body is light. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Qinpi (written 芩皮), also called qinpi. Shennong, Leigong, Huangdi, Qibo: sour, non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly [cold]. It may grow beside the waters of Yuanju. Collected in the second and eighth months (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Wu Pu* [probably an error for the *Mingyi*] says: Also called cenpi or shitan. It grows in Lujiang and Yuanju. The bark is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on qinpi (ash bark).
- The second "*Wu Pu* says" is probably an error for the *Mingyi*, judging from its content and the form of other entries (translated as in the base).
- In Wu Pu's text, 李氏：小 lacks a character.
