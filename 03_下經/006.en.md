# 神農本草經 下經 006 戎塩 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Rongyan (rock salt). Governs brightening the eyes and eye pain; it augments qi, firms the flesh and bones and removes toxic gu. Dayan (common salt) makes one vomit (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "governs bound heat in the intestines and stomach"; the *Daguan* edition has it in black characters). Luyan (bittern): bitter, cold; governs great heat, wasting-thirst and manic vexation, dispels pathogen and brings down gu toxins, and softens the flesh and skin (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "also called hanshi; it brightens the eyes and augments qi"). It is found in pools and marshes (formerly three kinds; now combined).

The *Mingyi* says: Rongyan, also called huyan, comes from Mount Huyan, Xiqiang, Beidi, and the south-east corner of Fulu City in Jiuquan. That from the northern sea is blue, that from the southern sea red. Collected in the tenth month. Dayan comes from Handan and Hedong. Luyan comes from the salt lakes of Hedong.

## Notes

The entry on rongyan (rock salt), with dayan and luyan appended.
- No flavor or nature is given for rongyan.
