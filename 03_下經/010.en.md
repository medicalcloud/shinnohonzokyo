# 神農本草經 下經 010 附子 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Fuzi (aconite lateral root). Acrid, warm. Governs wind-cold, cough with counterflow and evil qi; it warms the centre, [treats] metal wounds, breaks hard concretions, accumulations and blood conglomerations, and [treats] cold-damp with lameness (the *Taiping yulan* reads "wilting"), hypertonicity and knee pain with inability to walk (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "it is the chief of the hundred drugs"; the *Daguan* edition has it in black characters). It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Fuzi, also called jin. Shennong: acrid; Qibo, Leigong: sweet, toxic; Mr Li: bitter, toxic, very warm. It may grow in Guanghan. Collected in the eighth month. The skin is black and the flesh white (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: It grows in Jianwei and east of Guanghan. That collected in [winter] months is fuzi; that collected in spring is wutou (*Taiping yulan*).

## Notes

The entry on fuzi (aconite lateral root), the first lower-grade herb.
- 脚痛 in the base is emended to 膝痛 ("knee pain").
- 月採 in the *Mingyi* quotation seems to lack a character; "winter" is supplied following texts such as the *Zhenglei bencao* (冬月採為附子).
