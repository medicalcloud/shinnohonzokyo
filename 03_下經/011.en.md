# 神農本草經 下經 011 烏頭 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wutou (aconite main root). Acrid, warm. Governs wind-stroke and aversion to wind with shivering; it brings out sweat, dispels cold-damp impediment and cough with counterflow and rising qi, and breaks accumulations and cold and heat. Its juice, decocted, is called sheyang and kills birds and beasts. Also called xidu, jizi or wuhui. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Wutou, also called jin, qiandi, dugong, beifu (the *Taiping yulan* reads guofu) or gengzi. Shennong, Leigong, Tongjun, Huangdi: sweet, toxic. It begins to grow in the first month; the leaves are thick, the stems square and hollow, the leaves facing one another in fours, resembling mugwort. It also says: Wuhui: Shennong, Leigong, Tongjun, Huangdi: toxic; Mr Li: slightly cold. Collected in the tenth month. It is shaped like wutou, with two forks meeting like a crow's beak, hence the name wuhui ("crow's beak"). What it fears, abhors and is assisted by are all the same as wutou. Also called cezi or jin. Shennong, Qibo: very toxic; Mr Li: very cold. Collected in the eighth month and dried in the shade. It is the large horn of fuzi; what it fears and abhors is the same as fuzi (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: It grows in Langling. Collected in the first and second months and dried in the shade. Those three cun or longer are tianxiong.

## Notes

The entry on wutou (aconite main root), touching also on wuhui and cezi.
- 中内 in the base is emended to 中風 ("wind-stroke").
