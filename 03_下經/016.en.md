# 神農本草經 下經 016 大黄 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dahuang (daio; rhubarb). Bitter, cold. Governs bringing down static blood, blood blockage and cold and heat; it breaks concretions and accumulations, lodged fluids and retained food, flushes and cleanses the intestines and stomach, pushes out the old and brings in the new, frees water and grain [base: 水殺] (the *Taiping yulan* has 道 after this), regulates the center and digests food, and calms and harmonizes the five zang. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Dahuang, also called huangliang, huoshen or furu. Shennong, Leigong: bitter, toxic; Bian Que: bitter, non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly cold; it is the general within. It may grow in the north of Shu Commandery or in Longxi. In the second month it sprouts, putting out yellow-red leaves facing each other in fours, with yellow stems about three chi tall; in the third month the flowers are yellow, in the fifth month the fruit black. The root is collected in the third month; it has yellow juice; it is cut and dried in the shade (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called huangliang. It grows in Hexi and Longxi. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried over fire.

## Notes

The entry on dahuang (rhubarb).
- 水殺 is taken as an error for 水穀 ("water and grain"); the base is kept.
- Wu Pu's "the general within" is related to rhubarb's nickname "the general".
