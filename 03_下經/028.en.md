# 神農本草經 下經 028 甘遂 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Gansui (kansui root). Bitter, cold. Governs abdominal enlargement with mounting-conglomeration, abdominal fullness, puffy swelling of the face and eyes, and lodged fluids and retained food; it breaks hard concretions and accumulations and frees the pathways of water and grain. Also called zhutian. It grows in river valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Gansui, also called zhutian, baize, chongze, guichou, linggao, gangao or ganze. Shennong, Tongjun: bitter, toxic; Qibo, Leigong: toxic. It should be collected in the second and eighth months (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called gangao, linggao, lingze or chongze. It grows in Zhongshan. The root is collected in the second month and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on gansui (kansui), regarded as a strong purgative and diuretic.
