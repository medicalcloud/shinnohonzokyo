# 神農本草經 下經 036 貫衆 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Guanzhong (kanju; fern rhizome). Bitter, slightly cold. Governs pathogenic heat qi in the abdomen and the various toxins, and kills the three worms. Also called guanjie, guanqu, baitou (the *Taiping yulan* reads 白), hujuan or bianfu. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Guanzhong, also called guanlai, guanzhong (written 中), qumu, guanzhong (written 鍾), baiqin, yaozao, bianfu or huangzhong. Shennong, Qibo: bitter, toxic; Tongjun, Bian Que: bitter; one classic: sweet, toxic; Huangdi: salty, sour; one classic: bitter, non-toxic. The leaves are yellow and face each other in pairs; the stems grow with clustered black hairs. It does not age in winter or summer. Flowers in the fourth month, fruit in the eighth month, black, clustered and connected, growing sideways in curls. The root is collected in the third and eighth months, the leaves in the fifth month (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called boping or yaozao. It is called "grass owl's head". It grows on Mount Yuan, in Yuanju and on Mount Shaoshi. The root is collected in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade.
