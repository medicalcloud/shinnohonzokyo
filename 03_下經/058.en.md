# 神農本草經 下經 058 芫花 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yuanhua (genkwa flower). Acrid, warm. Governs cough with counterflow and rising qi, wheezing throat and panting, swollen throat and shortness of breath, gu toxins and ghost malaria, and mounting-conglomeration and welling-abscesses and swellings, and kills insects and fish. Also called qushui. It grows in river valleys (formerly in the tree section, which is wrong).

The *Wu Pu* says: Yuanhua, also called qushui, baihua, ercaogen or huangdaji. Shennong, Huangdi: toxic; Bian Que, Qibo: bitter; Mr Li: very cold. It sprouts in the second month; the leaves are blue-green and turn black when thicker. The flowers may be purple, red or white. When the fruit has all fallen in the third month, the leaves appear. The flowers are collected in the third and fifth months. The root of yuanhua is also called chiyuangen. Shennong, Leigong: bitter, toxic. It grows in Handan. Collected in the ninth and eighth months and dried in the shade. Taken long, it causes diarrhoea. It can be used to poison fish (*Taiping yulan*; also in an abridgement of the *Tujing*).

The *Mingyi* says: Also called duyu or duyuan. Its root, called shusang, can be used to poison fish. It grows at the source of the Huai. The flowers [base: 薬] are collected on the third day of the third month and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on yuanhua (genkwa flower), which Sun moved from the trees to the herbs.
- The Executive Yuan text of Sun's edition writes 芫華 (variant).
