# 神農本草經 下經 066 雷丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Leiwan (omphalia) (the *Taiping yulan* reads leigongwan). Bitter, cold. Governs killing the three worms; it drives out toxic qi and heat in the stomach, benefits men but not women. Made into a rubbing ointment, it dispels the hundred illnesses of children (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "also called leishi"; the *Daguan* edition has it in black characters). It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: Leiwan. Shennong: bitter; Huangdi, Qibo, Tongjun: sweet, toxic; Bian Que: sweet, non-toxic; Mr Li: very cold (the *Taiping yulan* quotes "also called leishi (written 実); it may grow in Hanzhong; collected in the eighth month").

The *Mingyi* says: Also called leishi or leishi (written 実). It grows in the soil of Shicheng and Hanzhong. The root is collected in the eighth month and dried in the sun.

## Notes

The entry on leiwan (a fungal sclerotium growing on bamboo roots). In the Zhongyi Jicheng text it has no heading and continues the preceding entry; it is separated as a unit.
- 治中 in the *Mingyi* quotation is taken as an error for 漢中.
