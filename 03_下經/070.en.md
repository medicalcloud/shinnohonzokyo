# 神農本草經 下經 070 黄環 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huanghuan. Bitter, neutral. Governs gu toxins, ghost infixation, ghostly demons and evil qi in the zang, and dispels cough with counterflow and cold and heat. Also called lingquan or dajiu. It grows in mountain valleys.

The *Wu Pu* says: [In] Shu, huanghuan, also called shengchu or genjiu. Shennong, Huangdi, Qibo, Tongjun, Bian Que: acrid; one classic: bitter, toxic. It sprouts in the second month; when first emerging it is bright red and two chi tall; the leaves are yellow with round tips, the stems large, the leaves with juice, yellowish-white. In the fifth month the fruit is round. The root is collected in the third month; it is yellow, with grain like the spokes of a wheel. It resolves and treats gu toxins (*Taiping yulan*).

The *Mingyi* says: It grows in Shu Commandery. The root is collected in the third month and dried in the shade.

## Notes

The entry on huanghuan; its identification is uncertain.
