# 神農本草經 下經 p023 夫大病之主，有中風傷寒，寒熱温瘧，中悪霍… (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The chief major illnesses are: wind-stroke and cold damage, cold and heat and warm malaria, malign stroke and cholera, abdominal enlargement and water swelling, intestinal afflux and diarrhoea, blockage of urine and stool, running piglet and rising qi, cough with counterflow and vomiting, jaundice and dispersion-thirst, lodged fluids and food aggregation, hard accumulations and concretions, fright evil and epilepsy, ghost infixation, throat impediment and toothache, deafness and blindness, metal wounds and fractures, welling-abscesses, swellings and malign sores, haemorrhoids, fistulas, goitres and tumours, the five taxations and seven damages with depletion and emaciation in men, vaginal discharge, flooding, blood blockage and genital erosion in women, and injuries from insects, snakes and gu toxins. These are the general principal signs; their variations and ramifications should each be dealt with according to their clues.

## Notes

A statement of the introduction listing the principal illnesses.
- 黄疽 is taken as 黄疸 ("jaundice"); the base is kept.
